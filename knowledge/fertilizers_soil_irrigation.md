# Soil, Fertilizer and Irrigation Guide — India

---

## SOIL HEALTH AND TESTING

### Why Soil Test?
- Soil testing every 2–3 years prevents over/under-fertilization
- Saves 20–30% fertilizer cost
- Identifies hidden deficiencies (zinc, sulphur, boron) causing hidden hunger

### How to Collect Soil Sample
1. Collect from 15–20 spots in the field in a W or zigzag pattern
2. Remove surface debris; dig 0–15 cm (for crops) or 0–30 cm (for orchards)
3. Mix all samples; take 500g composite sample
4. Do not collect from spots near bunds, waterlogged areas, or manure heaps
5. Submit to soil testing lab (state agriculture department or KVK — free in most states)
6. Soil Health Card scheme: free testing every 2 years; portal: soilhealth.dac.gov.in

### Understanding Soil Test Results
- Organic Carbon (OC): <0.5% low; 0.5–0.75% medium; >0.75% high
- Available N: <280 kg/ha low; 280–560 kg/ha medium; >560 kg/ha high
- Available P: <10 kg/ha low; 10–25 kg/ha medium; >25 kg/ha high
- Available K: <108 kg/ha low; 108–280 kg/ha medium; >280 kg/ha high

## COMMON FERTILIZERS

### Nitrogenous Fertilizers
- **Urea (46% N)**: Cheapest N source; apply in cool morning; do not apply before rain (loss)
- **DAP (18% N, 46% P)**: Good basal fertilizer for both N and P needs
- **CAN (26% N)**: Slower release; better for clayey soils

### Phosphatic Fertilizers
- **SSP (16% P, 11% S)**: Cheap; also supplies sulphur; good for sulphur-deficient soils
- **DAP (46% P, 18% N)**: Most popular; costly but concentrated

### Potassic Fertilizers
- **MOP (60% K)**: Standard potash; avoid in chloride-sensitive crops (potato)
- **SOP (50% K, 18% S)**: Better for potato, tobacco, fruits; expensive

### Micronutrients
- **Zinc (ZnSO4 21%)**: Most deficient in India; apply 25 kg/ha; yellowing between veins on young leaves
- **Boron (Borax 11%)**: Critical for fruit set and pollen germination; 10 kg/ha soil or 0.5g/L foliar
- **Sulphur (Gypsum/SSP)**: Needed for oilseeds, pulses, onion; pale yellowing of young leaves
- **Iron (FeSO4 19%)**: Interveinal chlorosis on young leaves; foliar spray 5g/L with lime
- **Manganese (MnSO4)**: Brown spots on leaves; foliar spray 0.5g/L

### Organic Inputs
- **FYM (Farmyard Manure)**: Apply 10–25 tonnes/ha; improves soil structure and water holding
- **Vermicompost**: Higher nutrients than FYM; 3–5 tonnes/ha
- **Trichoderma viride / harzianum**: Biocontrol + plant growth promoter; 2.5 kg/ha in soil
- **Rhizobium**: Seed inoculant for pulses and soybean; increases N-fixation by 30–40 kg/ha
- **PSB (Phosphate Solubilizing Bacteria)**: Seed inoculant; improves P availability

## PLANT NUTRITION DEFICIENCY SYMPTOMS

| Nutrient | Symptom | Where It Appears |
|----------|---------|-----------------|
| Nitrogen (N) | General yellowing | Older/lower leaves first |
| Phosphorus (P) | Purple/reddish colouring | Older leaves, stems |
| Potassium (K) | Scorching/browning of leaf edges | Older leaves |
| Zinc (Zn) | Interveinal chlorosis, small leaves, white bud | Young leaves |
| Iron (Fe) | Interveinal chlorosis (veins remain green) | Young leaves |
| Sulphur (S) | Uniform yellowing | Young leaves |
| Boron (B) | Distorted, thick leaves; poor fruit set | Growing points |
| Magnesium (Mg) | Interveinal chlorosis | Older leaves |

## IRRIGATION MANAGEMENT

### Methods
- **Flood/Furrow Irrigation**: Common; water-intensive; suited to flat fields and rice
- **Sprinkler Irrigation**: Good for wheat, vegetables, groundnut; 30–40% water saving vs flood
- **Drip Irrigation**: Best for orchards, sugarcane, vegetables; 50–60% water saving; highest yield; PMKSY subsidy available (contact state horticulture dept)
- **Fertigation**: Applying soluble fertilizers through drip system; 20–30% fertilizer saving

### Critical Irrigation Stages by Crop
| Crop | Critical Stages (Do Not Miss) |
|------|-------------------------------|
| Wheat | CRI (21 DAS), Tillering, Jointing, Flowering, Grain fill |
| Rice | Transplanting, Tillering, Panicle initiation, Flowering |
| Cotton | Squaring, Flowering, Boll development |
| Soybean | Flowering, Pod fill |
| Mustard | Branching (25–30 DAS), Pod fill (70–75 DAS) |
| Potato | Tuber initiation, Tuber bulking |
| Tomato | Transplanting, Flowering, Fruit development |
| Onion | Bulb development (60–90 DAT) |

### Water Stress Signs
- Wilting in afternoon — mild stress
- Wilting in morning — severe stress; yield loss already happening
- Blue-green leaf colour in maize — early stress indicator
- Drought-stressed crops are more susceptible to pest and disease attack

### PM Krishi Sinchai Yojana (PMKSY)
- Government subsidy on drip and sprinkler irrigation
- 55–75% subsidy for small/marginal farmers; 45–55% for others
- Apply through state horticulture/agriculture department
- Also covers water harvesting structures under PMKSY-WDC

## SOIL PREPARATION

### Deep Ploughing
- Do once every 3–5 years; breaks hardpan; improves root depth
- Best done in summer (April–May) before kharif

### Green Manuring
- Grow dhaincha (Sesbania), sunhemp, or cowpea; incorporate before flowering
- Adds 60–80 kg N/ha equivalent; improves organic matter

### Soil pH Correction
- Acidic soil (pH <6): Apply agricultural lime (CaCO3) @ 2–4 tonnes/ha
- Alkaline/Saline soil (pH >8.5): Apply gypsum (CaSO4) @ 5–10 tonnes/ha; grow tolerant crops (barley, cotton)
- Normal range for most crops: 6.0–7.5
